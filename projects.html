Vector Autoregression (VAR): Inflation, Unemployment and Monetary Policy

↗

Replication of Stock and Watson (2001) using a quarterly U.S. Vector Autoregression, VAR(4), on inflation, unemployment and monetary policy. Applies Granger causality tests, impulse response functions, forecast error variance decomposition, structural identification and out-of-sample forecasting.

PythonVector AutoregressionGranger CausalityImpulse Response FunctionsStructural IdentificationOut-of-Sample Forecasting

Local Projections: Monetary Policy Transmission

↗

An independent Python replication of Figures 5 and 6 in Jordà and Taylor’s “Local Projections” (2025). Estimates monetary policy effects on U.S. prices and unemployment with instrumental-variable local projections (LP-IV), coding two-stage least squares and Newey-West inference from scratch, validated against Stata.

PythonLocal ProjectionsLP-IV2SLSNewey-West HACWald TestsStata Validation

Forecasting Industrial Production with Ridge and Lasso Regression

↗

Forecasting U.S. industrial production growth with Ridge, Lasso and regression trees using 120+ FRED-MD indicators. Tuned with time-series cross-validation and evaluated out-of-sample against autoregressive AR(2) and historical-mean benchmarks at 1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month horizons.

Minimum Wage and Employment

↗

Python replication of Card and Krueger’s (1994) study of minimum wages and labor demand. Estimates difference-in-differences (DID) and ordinary least squares (OLS) models using wage-gap measures, with robustness checks on alternative full-time-equivalent (FTE) employment specifications.

PythonDifference-in-DifferencesOLSWage-Gap MeasuresFTE Robustness Checks

CRA Designation and Mortgage Lending

↗

An independent Python analysis of whether the Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) label for tracts below 80% of area median family income shifts home-purchase lending. Uses 2023 Home Mortgage Disclosure Act data, regression discontinuity design, placebo cutoffs and a bank versus non-bank split.

PythonRegression Discontinuity DesignPlacebo CutoffsHMDAFFIEC Census Data

Automobile Demand with Instrumental Variables

↗

Independent analysis of U.S. automobile demand using 1971–1990 Berry, Levinsohn and Pakes (BLP) data. Compares two-stage least squares (2SLS), limited information maximum likelihood (LIML) and OLS to correct price endogeneity, with first-stage F-statistics, weak-instrument and overidentification tests.

Python2SLSLIMLFirst-Stage F-StatisticsWeak IV TestsOveridentification Tests
